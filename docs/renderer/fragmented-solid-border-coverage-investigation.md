# Fractional solid-border coverage in fragmented layouts

Chromium remains the sole pixel target. This investigation used the unchanged
four-profile oracle and the clean [v26 census](generated/four-profile-census-v26.json)
as the baseline. The diagnostic renderer change described below was reverted.

## Reproducer and observation

`wpt/css_break/block-max-height-001` places a 50 × 200 CSS-pixel child with
`border: solid` inside three columns over a yellow background. At
1280 × 720 CSS pixels and 1.25 device scale, the child's long right border
has an edge cell at physical x=66. At `(66, 50)`, Open UI stores
`(64, 64, 0, 255)` and Chromium stores `(63, 63, 0, 255)`. The same family is
exact at 1× and 2×, but differs at 1.25× and 1.5×. In the baseline,
`block-max-height-001` has 1,287 differing pixels at 1.25× and 1,590 at
1.5×. The repeated edge cells account for most of them; fragment-end rows
have separate, larger color differences.

The nested child uses the ordinary uniform solid-border path, even when its
parent is fragmented. A first diagnostic that applied physical coverage only
when `Fragment::fragmentation_writing_direction` was set changed no pixels in
the 14 `block-max-height` and `block-min-height` cases tested. Applying the
physical-coverage rectangle path to all opaque, square uniform solid borders
reduced `block-max-height-001` to 340 and 528 differing pixels, respectively,
but introduced 44 new wrong pixels in the 2-pixel orange border of
`block-max-height-004` at 1.25×. A diagnostic that changed the packing at
half-pixel phases made the family worse at 1.5×.

## Broader guard rejected the candidate

A third diagnostic applied physical-coverage rectangles only to opaque,
square, uniform solid borders at least 3 CSS pixels wide. The 14-case sample
improved at fractional scales without a sample regression. The complete
`css_break` inventory (1,220 cases × four profiles) rejected that rule:

| Profile | Baseline exact | Diagnostic exact | Baseline differing pixels | Diagnostic differing pixels |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 800×600@1 | 1,213 | 1,213 | 454 | 454 |
| 375×667@2 | 1,185 | 1,185 | 28,533 | 28,533 |
| 1280×720@1.25 | 933 | 898 | 106,289 | 136,295 |
| 1920×1080@1.5 | 1,056 | 1,035 | 358,589 | 379,654 |
| **Total** | **4,387** | **4,331** | **493,865** | **544,936** |

Across the 4,880 comparisons, 33 improved and 85 regressed by differing-pixel
count. One became exact and 57 formerly exact comparisons became different.
The diagnostic had zero render errors. All 4,880 Chromium oracle identities
and decoded image hashes matched the clean baseline. The diagnostic report was
generated by `run_renderer_matrix.py` from dirty source and is not release
qualification evidence.

Pinned Chromium 147's `BoxBorderPainter::PaintBorderFastPath` calls
`DrawSolidBorderRect` for a uniform square solid border. That function draws
one antialiased stroked rectangle for every border width. A threshold at
3 CSS pixels has no matching branch in that source. The next repair must
explain the renderer's stroke geometry and coverage across widths, phases,
backgrounds, and fragmentation before changing this shared path. The
remaining mismatch belongs to `openui-paint`; its exact root cause is open.
